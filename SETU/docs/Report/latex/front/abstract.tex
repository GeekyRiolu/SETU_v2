\setlength{\parindent}{0.5in}

India recognises twenty-two scheduled languages, yet almost every machine
translation service that serves them well is a cloud service, which excludes
precisely the users who need translation most: a health worker in a village
with no signal, a citizen at a gram panchayat counter. It also means every
sentence a user types leaves their device, while
running a large model locally fails on arithmetic, since systems such as
IndicTrans2 hold billions of parameters. \setu{} (Sanskrit
\dev{सेतु}, ``bridge'') closes that gap by distilling a strong teacher into
compact students that run entirely on the device. The project began from the
hypothesis that \emph{preference distillation}, ranking teacher hypotheses by
chrF and training with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), would beat
sequence-level knowledge distillation for tiny models. A controlled head-to-head
at matched student size refuted it: at 100{,}000 training sentences,
sequence-level knowledge distillation (\seqkd{}) on the teacher's one-best
output reached \textbf{17.09 BLEU} against \textbf{11.10 BLEU} for
reference-trained DPO, a gain of roughly 54\,\%. The
bottleneck was never the optimisation objective but noise in the mined human
references, so the system was redirected to \seqkd{} and DPO retained as a
reported negative result.

The delivered system trains one 52.6-million-parameter Marian student per
direction on about 497{,}000 teacher-distilled pairs and quantises it to INT4
ONNX at \textbf{103.9\,MB}, a fourfold compression; forty-four directions
covering all twenty-two scheduled languages both ways were trained on an NVIDIA
A40 vGPU. Against the teacher on a held-out Samanantar slice, \textbf{14 of 22
evaluated directions meet the $\geq 0.80$ student/teacher BLEU ratio target},
with a mean of \textbf{0.838} and a best of \textbf{0.955}
(Marathi\arrowto{}English). The other three targets pass without exception:
103.9\,MB against a 200\,MB budget, 105--202\,ms mean latency against 500\,ms,
and inference with every network socket disabled. An English-pivot router
chains two students where no direct model exists, so 44 models serve 506
directions. The system ships as a FastAPI service, an offline progressive web
application, a Next.js client and a command-line tool over one shared inference
engine, validated by 85 automated tests.

\vspace{6pt}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Neural Machine Translation; Knowledge Distillation;
Direct Preference Optimization; Indic Languages; On-Device Inference;
Quantisation.
